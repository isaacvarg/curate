# curate

This is curate's canonical brand: its colours, type, spacing, shapes and components, and the reasons behind them. The tokens in the front matter are normative. The shared package's tokens are generated from them and are never edited by hand. How each platform applies the brand lives elsewhere: MOBILE-DESIGN.md for the app (#37), and a web equivalent with the first web UI ticket. Terms in **bold** are defined in [GLOSSARY.md](GLOSSARY.md).

To change the design, edit this file, run `npx @google/design.md lint DESIGN.md`, and regenerate the tokens.

## Overview

**Quiet editorial, for a hobby you keep for yourself.** curate is a personal app for collecting, rating and journaling hobbies, from board games and beer to books, tinned fish and TV. It is used one-handed at the table, mid-game or mid-tasting. It should feel calm and unhurried, like a well-kept field journal of your own life: never sterile, never a bank, never a game that nags you.

Three ideas carry the look:

1. **The interface steps back.** Ink on warm linen does the work, and the only colour comes from the user's own photos and their **Collections**' accents.
2. **Two voices of type.** A soft serif (Fraunces) speaks for the user's things: **Item** names, the **Verdict**, notes and section heads. A plain sans (Figtree) runs the controls, the numbers and the metadata.
3. **Honest states.** Unrated never looks like a low rating, and an incomplete total never looks final. Dashes, words and muted ink mark "not yet" throughout.

**Identity.** There is no logo or symbol, because "curate" is a placeholder name. The only mark is the lowercase word "curate", set in the `wordmark` style. It appears in the app bar and small on **Rating Cards**.

**How this direction was chosen.** It started from the brief in [docs/design/brand-exploration-prompts.md](docs/design/brand-exploration-prompts.md). Three directions were explored, and Linen & Moss (a field journal) was chosen as the base. It was then mixed with Google Stitch's Stone & Ink in two review rounds:

- **Kept from Linen & Moss:**
  - its warmth;
  - italic serif section heads;
  - pill buttons;
  - the Collection spine;
  - a header wash in the Collection's tint.
- **Taken from Stitch:**
  - tonal cards with a crisp hairline;
  - small-caps labels;
  - tinted Collection badges;
  - its Log Session, Score Card, Journal and Rating Card layouts.
- **Corrected along the way:**
  - The neutrals moved halfway towards warm stone. Linen's green tint made plum and rose look off and turned dark mode olive-drab.
  - Fraunces dropped to half softness, so it ages better.
  - Amber was re-tuned for dark mode.

## Colors

The palette has three parts: warm neutrals, ink, and the Collection accents.

- **Neutrals** are warm linen with a faint green tint.
  - **Ground** (`#F0EDE3` / `#1B1B17`) is the page.
  - **Surface** (`#F7F5ED` / `#24241E`) is a card, one step lighter in light mode and one step raised in dark.
  - **Sunk** (`#E6E2D7` / `#141411`) is a well: score cells, Session tiles, toggle tracks.
  - **Line** and **line-strong** are hairlines and outlines.
  - Pure `#000` and `#FFF` never appear.
- **Ink is the primary colour** (`primary`, the same value as `ink`). Primary buttons, the active tab, focus rings and body text are all ink. There is no brand hue, so a screen full of Collections never has two colours competing for "brand".
- **Ink has three tiers.** Use `ink` for content, `ink-muted` for metadata and labels, and `ink-faint` for placeholders and empty stars. All three pass WCAG AA (at least 4.5:1) on ground, surface and sunk, in both modes.
- **Collection accents** are eight muted hues: moss, amber, slate, coral, plum, teal, olive and rose. A Collection owns one. It colours that Collection's filled stars, selected chips, its spine, its badge and its header wash. Accents only ever mark data. Chrome never uses them.
  - Each accent clears 4.6:1 (light) or 4.8:1 (dark) on ground, surface and sunk. That's enough to work as text, and enough for `ground` text on an accent fill: a selected chip puts the ground colour on the accent.
  - Each accent comes with three derived tones:
    - **`-tint`** (12% of the accent in ground) is the badge fill, the Score Card header and the header wash.
    - **`-text`** (the accent pulled 25% towards ink) is accent text on a tint, which keeps AA there.
    - **`-wash`** (6% of the accent in surface) lights the active Participant's column on a Score Card.

The default assignment in examples is Board Games → moss, Beers I've Had → amber, Books → slate, Tinned Fish → coral and TV → plum. These are only examples: no design may depend on a particular hue.

## Light and dark

The format has no modes yet, so this section records the convention. **Every colour has a light value under its plain name and a dark value under the same name plus `-dark`.** Components follow the same pattern (`card` and `card-dark`), which lets the linter check contrast in both modes. The two modes are equals. The app follows the system setting, and neither mode is derived from the other by inversion.

Dark mode is a warm charcoal, not black. In dark mode:
- surface is raised above ground and sunk is darker than it, so cards lift and wells recess;
- ink is a warm off-white;
- the primary button inverts to an off-white pill with charcoal text;
- accents are lighter versions of the same hues, fitted to the same contrast targets.

## Collection accents

This section records an unknown field. **The rule that turns any colour into a Collection accent.** Users will be able to pick their own colour later, so the eight curated accents are inputs to a rule, not a closed list. Given a hue H and a chroma C, in OKLCH:

1. **Light value:** step the lightness down from 0.75 until the colour reaches 4.6:1 against ground, surface and sunk (light). Scale C by 1.1.
2. **Dark value:** step the lightness up from 0.45 until it reaches 4.8:1 against the dark ground, surface and sunk. Scale C by 1.05. Yellows and oranges, like amber, may take more chroma in dark mode so a filled chip doesn't look muddy.
3. **Derive** `-tint`, `-text` and `-wash` in OKLab exactly as described in Colors.

The curated hues and chromas are:

| Accent | Hue | Chroma |
|---|---|---|
| moss | 140 | 0.085 |
| amber | 72 | 0.10 (dark: 0.125 at hue 68) |
| slate | 252 | 0.07 |
| coral | 38 | 0.105 |
| plum | 335 | 0.085 |
| teal | 198 | 0.07 |
| olive | 108 | 0.085 |
| rose | 8 | 0.085 |

Two pairs sit close together: amber and olive, and coral and rose. Don't give neighbouring Collections a close pair by default.

The custom-colour feature implements this rule in code, so a user's hex is fitted the same way. A user never picks a raw hex that skips the fitting.

## Typography

Two typefaces, both from Google Fonts under open licences that allow bundling them in the app.

- **Fraunces** is the display serif, always at `"SOFT" 50, "WONK" 0`. It is soft enough to feel friendly and plain enough not to date. Fraunces sets:
  - screen titles (`display`) and Item, Occasion and Participant names (`heading`);
  - the Verdict readout (`verdict`, `verdict-sm`) and Score Card category names (`category`);
  - the `wordmark`.
- **Figtree** is the interface sans. It sets body text, controls, metadata and every number that changes. Scores, counts, totals and times use `number`, or any style with `"tnum"` and `"lnum"`, so columns line up.

**Two heading levels.** This is the rule that gives curate its voice.

1. **Italic serif heads** (`section-italic`, set in Fraunces *italic*) name a screen's big sections: "*Your Collections*", "*Overall Rating*", "*Expansions*", "*Recent Sessions*", "*Participants*", and the days in the Journal ("*Today*", "*Saturday*").
2. **Small-caps labels** (`label-caps`, set in uppercase) name things inside a section:
   - Attribute keys (PLAYERS, DESIGNER, ABV);
   - field groups (OBSERVATIONS, VERDICT, NOTE);
   - Score Card column heads (CATEGORY);
   - states (OPEN OCCASION, IN BREWERY DAY);
   - Collection badges.

The format has no `fontStyle` or `textTransform`, so the token names carry them. `section-italic`, `note-italic`, `byline-italic` and `caption-italic` are always italic, and `label-caps` is always uppercase.

**Italic is otherwise reserved for the user's own words and quiet secondary lines.** That means notes (`note-italic`), a maker or author under a title (`byline-italic`, as in "*Fremont Brewing*" or "*Susanna Clarke*"), the "last logged" line on a Collection card, and Media captions (`caption-italic`). Titles in the app bar are upright.

**Sentence case everywhere else.** Titles, body text and buttons are always sentence case. Buttons are never all-caps.

## Layout

The phone comes first, designed at 390 × 844pt.

- **Margins:** 20pt at the sides of the screen (`margin`), 32pt between sections (`section`), and 16pt inside cards (`card-padding`). Spacing steps through 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 40, 56 and 80.
- **One-handed reach:**
  - The primary action sits in a dock at the bottom of the screen, in thumb reach, and says what it does in context ("Log a Session *in Brewery day*").
  - Main navigation is four tabs below the dock: Collections, Journal, Search, You.
  - Secondary actions sit in the dock beside the primary action (a camera button) or below it as a quiet text button ("Save draft").
- **Touch targets:** the Verdict stars and choice chips are 48pt (`touch-target`). Nothing tappable is under 44pt (`touch-target-min`).
- **Photos:**
  - User photos are large and run the full width of the column on Item and Log Session.
  - Photos never have text, badges, gradients or filters over them. Attributes and badges sit below a photo.
  - On Log Session, the photo is about 160pt tall and shrinks into the header as you scroll, so the Verdict and chips stay reachable.
- **Header wash:** on an Item, a Session or a Score Card, the top of the screen is washed in the Collection's `-tint`, which fades into ground by the title (about 320pt down). It's the only screen-level use of Collection colour.

## Elevation & Depth

Flat. Hierarchy comes from tone, hairlines and space, not shadows.

- **Cards** are a tonal panel (surface) with a 1pt hairline (`line`) and the `card` radius. Cards group things that act as a unit:
  - a Collection;
  - the Open Occasion;
  - an Item's Attributes, its Overall Rating, and its Recent Sessions;
  - each Participant on Log Session;
  - the Score Card table;
  - each Occasion in the Journal;
  - the Rating Card.
- **Wells** (sunk) recess the things you type into or tap through: score cells, Session tiles in the Journal, the track behind toggles.
- **Hairlines** separate rows inside a card: Rating field rows and list rows.
- **Shadows** appear only on things that float above the page, like bottom sheets and menus. They use one soft shadow (`0 12px 32px` at 18% black) and nothing else.

## Shapes

- **Pills** (`pill`) for buttons, choice chips, Participant chips, badges, the Verdict's ordered-labels track, and the Rating Card size switcher. The pill is the one shape you can tap.
- **Cards** have a 14pt radius (`card`), photos 12pt (`photo`), thumbnails 10pt (`thumb`), and fields and score cells 8pt (`field`).
- **The Collection spine** is a 5pt bar of the accent down the left edge of a Collection card, running the card's full height. It has no radius of its own, because the card clips it.
- **Dashes mean "not yet":**
  - an unrated Verdict's star outlines;
  - a Score Card cell that hasn't been entered (dashed in the accent);
  - the placement slots while a total is incomplete;
  - a Participant who left, shown as a dashed chip.

## Components

Each component has a light token and a `-dark` token. The examples use moss. The `collection-badge-*` and `choice-chip-selected-*` tokens exist for all eight accents in both modes, so each accent's pairing is lint-checked.

### Verdict control

The Verdict is the bottom-line Rating, and the most-touched control in the app. One family, three forms, all filling with the Collection accent:

- **Stars** (the default):
  - Five thin-outline stars, each a 48pt target (`verdict-star`). Tap either half of a star for a half step, or slide across them.
  - Filled stars use the accent and empty stars `ink-faint`.
  - Beside the stars, the value in the serif at `verdict` size on an Item's Overall Rating and `verdict-sm` elsewhere, written as a fraction: "4½ of 5".
- **Numeric** (/10, /100): a large serif number (`verdict-numeric`) with a pill stepper beside it.
- **Ordered labels** (tiers, thumbs): a segmented pill track on sunk (`verdict-labels`). The chosen label fills with the accent (`verdict-labels-selected`).
- **Unrated** is its own state in every form:
  - stars have dashed outlines and the words "Not rated" (`verdict-unrated`);
  - the numeric form shows a dashed box with "Not rated";
  - the labels track is dashed.
  
  It is never shown as zero stars or 0.
- **Every step gives a light haptic tick**, and the fill animates (see Motion).
- **Read-only stars** are 16pt (or 20pt on a Rating Card), with the same glyph. A Participant card's header shows them once that Participant has a Verdict.

### Rating field rows

One opinion field per 48pt row (`rating-field-row`), separated by hairlines inside the Overall Rating card. Each row shows:
- the field name;
- a **Shared Field** badge (`shared-field-badge`: a small outlined pill reading "Shared"), only when the field has a Shared Field identity;
- the scale as small pips filled with the accent;
- the value in tabular figures ("4/5").

An empty field shows a dash. The card ends with one standing line, such as "**Top 10% of your games** · 23 Sessions · You win 52%".

### Choice chips

Choice chips are used for Observations and multiple choice.
- **Size and layout:** 48pt pills (`choice-chip`), laid out in a two-column grid so the targets are even. They fall back to wrapping when a label is too long for half the width.
- **Unselected:** an outline, with a `+` at the right.
- **Selected:** fills with the accent, with ground text and a `✓` (`choice-chip-selected-*`). The tick means the state never relies on colour alone.

### Condition-builder row

This is the row for "when [Attribute] [is] [value]" in Level settings (`condition-builder-row`).
- **Pickers:** each picker is an outlined 44pt field (`condition-picker`) with a chevron.
- **Group toggle:** above a group of rows, a segmented **all of / any of** toggle (`match-toggle`, with `match-toggle-selected` for the chosen side).
- **What it can reference:** conditions look only at Attributes, never Tags.

### Collection badge and spine

- **Badge.** Wherever an Item names its Collection (the Item header, a Journal Occasion, the Rating Card), it shows a tinted pill badge (`collection-badge-*`). The badge has a `-tint` fill, `-text` lettering in `label-caps`, and a hairline in the accent.
- **Spine.** Collection cards carry the spine instead (`collection-spine`).

### Score Card

The densest screen, and it has to stay calm.

- **The table:**
  - It sits on one card (`card`, no padding), under a header row tinted with the Collection's `-tint` (`score-sheet-header`).
  - Category names are in the serif (`score-sheet-category`).
  - Score cells are filled wells (`score-cell`), with right-aligned tabular figures.
- **The active Participant's column** is lit with `-wash` (`score-column-active`). Its header carries an accent dot and "Entering", so the one phone can be passed around the table.
- **A cell that hasn't been entered** is dashed in the accent on surface (`score-cell-missing`), showing "—". It is the only accent on the table, so it's found at once.
- **An incomplete total** is a running sum in muted sans with a trailing "+", labelled "So far, without Round goals" (`score-total-incomplete`). A final total is set in the serif (`score-total-final`). The placement slots stay dashed until every column is in.
- **Below the table:**
  - A "Pass the phone to Sam" card says what's missing ("Only Round goals are left. Placement is incomplete until Sam's are in.") and holds the three placement slots.
  - The dock's primary action names the next step ("Enter Round goals for Sam"), with "Save draft" below it.
- **No icons** on categories.

### Lists, tiles and placement

- **List rows** inside a card are at least 60pt tall, with a chevron at the right. An Expansion row leads with a thumbnail.
- **Recent Sessions** lead with a placement circle (`placement-circle`, 40pt). It shows "1st" in `-text` on `-tint`, or sunk and muted ink for other places.
- **Journal Session tiles** are sunk wells holding the Item name, read-only stars and the time.

### Buttons, tabs and the dock

- **Primary:** an ink pill (`button-primary`), 52pt tall.
- **Secondary:** an outlined pill on ground (`button-secondary`).
- **Text button:** an ink label with no fill, at least 44pt tall.
- **Tabs:** `tab` and `tab-active`, with an icon above a micro label. The active tab is ink and semibold, and the others are `ink-faint`.

### Screen patterns

- **Collections home:**
  - The Open Occasion card comes first (OPEN OCCASION, its name, location marked private, Participants, End Occasion).
  - Then a card per Collection: spine, name, latest Item, an italic "last logged" line, and the count with a small-caps unit (ITEMS).
- **Item:**
  - Photo, title, Collection badge and Level.
  - An Attributes card, then the Overall Rating card.
  - Expansions, then Recent Sessions.
  - One "Log a Session", which opens the Score Card when the Item has one.
- **Log Session:**
  - A state line (IN BREWERY DAY · today), the badge, the title, and the byline.
  - The photo, with the Attributes below it.
  - Participant chips. A Participant who left is a dashed chip ("*Sam left early*" with `+`), and tapping it adds them back.
  - A card per Participant: Observations, a full-width Verdict row, and a note.
- **Journal:**
  - "On this day" first.
  - Then each day under an italic head, with an Occasion card holding a horizontal photo strip with italic captions, Session tiles, and "Log next beer in this Occasion".
- **Rating Card:**
  - The card: photo, serif title with the stars on the right, byline, italic note, field pips on a sunk strip, the Collection badge, and the wordmark at the bottom right.
  - Below the card: a size switcher (Postcard 1:1, Story 9:16, Index Slip 3:2) and the "On the card" chips.

## Do's and Don'ts

- **Do** use the glossary's words exactly:
  - Collection, Item, Level, Attribute, Session, Occasion, Open Occasion, Journal, Verdict, Overall Rating, Rating, Observation, Score, Score Card, Participant, Guest, Media, Rating Card.
  - Not "check-in", "log entry", "score" for an opinion, "event", "archive", "catalogue" or "curator".
- **Do** keep ink as the only chrome colour, and let accents mark data.
- **Do** use the two heading levels: an italic serif head for a section, and small caps for labels inside it.
- **Do** give every changing number tabular figures.
- **Do** keep primary actions in the bottom dock, and keep the Verdict and chips at 48pt.
- **Do** show "not yet" with dashes and words.
- **Don't** put text, badges or gradients on a user's photo.
- **Don't** show unrated as zero stars or 0.
- **Don't** show an incomplete total in the style of a final one.
- **Don't** use pure `#000` or `#FFF`, a brand hue, or a raw hex that skips the accent rule.
- **Don't** add shadows to anything that doesn't float, and don't add icons to Score Card categories or Session tiles.
- **Don't** set buttons in all-caps, and don't use emoji in the interface.
- **Don't** cheerlead or nag. That rules out streaks, "Awesome! 🎉", and stats nobody asked for.
- **Don't** invent fields for decoration, such as catalogue numbers.

## Motion

This section records an unknown field. Use a little motion, and only where it has a purpose:
- the Verdict's fill slides across the stars in 240ms;
- selected chips fill in 240ms;
- Score totals tick up when they change;
- sheets settle with a soft spring that doesn't overshoot;
- buttons scale to 0.98 when pressed (120ms).

The easing is `cubic-bezier(0.22, 0.61, 0.36, 1)`. Confirmations are haptics, not animations. Reduced motion turns off every transition.

## Iconography

This section records an unknown field. One thin-stroke set: Lucide at a 1.5px stroke, 22pt (16pt inline). Icons are never filled, and the star is the only glyph that fills, when it shows a Verdict. Icons label actions and navigation. They don't decorate data.

## Labels

This section records an unknown field. Printed QR **Labels** and ex libris are monochrome, in ink on white stock, so they print on any printer. The "curate" wordmark is optional on them.
